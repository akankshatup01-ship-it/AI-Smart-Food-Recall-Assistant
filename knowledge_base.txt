FOOD RECALL KNOWLEDGE BASE

ALLERGEN RECALLS

Food products may be recalled when an allergen is present but is not correctly declared on the product label.

Common allergens include milk, peanuts, tree nuts, wheat, soy, egg, fish, shellfish, and sesame.

For an allergen-related recall:
- Verify the affected product.
- Verify the affected lot or batch code.
- Verify the recall number.
- Review the reason for recall.
- Review ingredient and allergen labeling.
- Identify the distribution area.
- Segregate affected inventory for review.
- Final recall decisions require human review.

UNDECLARED MILK

A product may be recalled when milk or a milk-derived ingredient is present but is not declared on the label.

For undeclared milk:
- Verify affected lots.
- Review ingredient and allergen labeling.
- Check the recall reason.
- Review distribution information.
- Do not assume every product from the same company is affected.

PEANUT RECALLS

Peanut-related recalls may involve undeclared peanut or peanut-derived ingredients.

For peanut recalls:
- Verify the product description.
- Check affected batch or lot codes.
- Review the recall reason.
- Review distribution information.
- Keep affected inventory separated until evaluated.

MICROBIAL CONTAMINATION

Food recalls may involve pathogens such as Salmonella, Listeria monocytogenes, or E. coli.

For contamination-related recalls:
- Verify the affected product and lot.
- Review the recall reason.
- Review distribution information.
- Follow applicable food safety procedures.
- Human review is required for final operational decisions.

TRACEABILITY

Food recall traceability information may include:
- Recalling firm
- Product description
- Recall number
- Batch or lot code
- Product quantity
- Recall classification
- Recall status
- Recall initiation date
- Firm location
- Distribution pattern

FDA distribution information does not necessarily provide individual customer shipment transactions.

RECALL CLASSIFICATION

Class I:
A situation where use of or exposure to the product may cause serious adverse health consequences or death.

Class II:
A situation where use of or exposure to the product may cause temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences, or where the probability of serious adverse health consequences is remote.

Class III:
A situation where use of or exposure to the product is not likely to cause adverse health consequences.
